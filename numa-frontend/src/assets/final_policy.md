# St Theresa's School (Plimmerton) Governance Policy Document

## Introduction

This Governance Policy Document outlines the framework by which St Theresa's School (Plimmerton) is governed. It reflects our commitment to strategic leadership, clarity of roles, policy-driven governance, and accountability to our community and the Crown. As a state-integrated Catholic primary school, our policies are shaped by our mission "To live, learn and love with Jesus, being the best we can be" and our SPARK values (Self-control, Positivity, Aroha, Respect, Kindness).

This document is structured around four main policy areas: Impact Policies, Board-Management Relationship Policies, Operational Expectation Policies, and Governance Culture Policies. These policies collectively define the desired results for our students, the relationship between the board and the principal, the boundaries for school operations, and how the board conducts itself.

Our policies are designed to ensure that we fulfil our obligations under Te Tiriti o Waitangi, the Education and Training Act 2020, and other relevant legislation. They also reflect our commitment to providing an inclusive, safe, and high-quality learning environment for our diverse student body, including our Māori (16%) and Samoan (8%) students.

This document serves as a guide for board members, staff, and the wider school community, ensuring transparency in our governance processes and clarity in our expectations and goals. It is a living document, subject to regular review and update to ensure it continues to meet the evolving needs of our school and community.

## Definitions

**Board**: The governing body of St Theresa's School (Plimmerton)

**Principal**: The chief executive of the school, responsible for its day-to-day management

**SPARK values**: Self-control, Positivity, Aroha, Respect, Kindness - the core values of St Theresa's School

**Impact Policies**: Policies that define the desired results for students and the school's purpose

**Operational Expectation Policies**: Policies that set boundaries and expectations for school operations

**Governance Culture Policies**: Policies that describe how the board will conduct itself and carry out its duties

**Board-Management Relationship Policies**: Policies that outline the relationship between the board and the principal

**Te Tiriti o Waitangi**: The Māori text of the Treaty of Waitangi

**Kāhui Ako**: Community of Learning, a group of education and training providers working together to help learners achieve their full potential

**PCBU**: Person Conducting a Business or Undertaking, as defined in the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015

**Crown**: The New Zealand Government

**Presiding Member (PM)**: The chairperson of the school board

# Impact Policies

## 1. Global Impact Policy

Each student will be enabled to develop their personality, talents and abilities to fullest potential, and participate as responsible, informed, active and critical citizens of the local, national and global communities in which they live - in a learning community characterised by respect for everyone's rights and Te Tiriti o Waitangi. The school will give effect to the National Education and Learning Priorities (NELP) in its policies and practices.[1][2]

## 1.1 Fulfilment of Educational Potential

At St Theresa's School (Plimmerton), each student will develop, to a level suitable for a successful transition to the next stage of their lives:

a) Resilience, determination, confidence, and creative and critical thinking, in line with our SPARK values (Self-control, Positivity, Aroha, Respect, Kindness).
b) Good social skills and the ability to form good relationships, embodying our gospel values of manaakitanga and whanaungatanga.
c) Participation in community life and fulfilment of civic and social responsibilities, as active members of our local Plimmerton community and the wider Porirua area.
d) Preparedness for future learning and challenges, developing as autonomous learners within our holistic educational framework.
e) Appreciation of the importance of:
   i. The inclusion of different groups and persons with different personal characteristics, reflecting our diverse student body (16% Māori, 8% Samoan, and others).
   ii. Diversity, cultural knowledge, identity, and the different official languages of New Zealand.
   iii. Te Tiriti o Waitangi and te reo Māori, honouring our commitment to biculturalism and the unique place of Māori in our school and society.
   iv. Addressing the needs of students with differing abilities, including those with disabilities, ensuring all students have the opportunity to achieve educational success.[3]

## 1.2 Experience of being in a Learning Community

At St Theresa's School (Plimmerton):

a) All members of the learning community value learning and human rights and support the learning and rights of others, in alignment with our mission "To live, learn and love with Jesus, being the best we can be."
b) Each student experiences respect for their rights to a suitable education, physical and emotional safety, non-discrimination, and other rights, within our Catholic educational environment.
c) Students engage in collaborative learning experiences that foster a sense of belonging and community, consistent with our participation in the Northern Porirua Kāhui Ako|Community of Learning.
d) The school takes all reasonable steps to eliminate racism, stigma, bullying, and any other forms of discrimination within the school community.[4]

## 1.3 Respect for Te Tiriti o Waitangi

Students at St Theresa's School (Plimmerton) will acknowledge, understand and respect:

a) New Zealand's bicultural heritage and the place of Te Tiriti as the founding document in the constitution.
b) Governance of New Zealand under the Crown.
c) Māori rights and interests under Article 2, with particular focus on local iwi and their significance to our school community.
d) Citizenship equality under Article 3, promoting unity and respect among all students regardless of their cultural background.
e) The importance of te reo Māori and tikanga Māori in our school life, integrating these elements into our daily practices and curriculum.
f) The school's commitment to taking all reasonable steps to make instruction available in tikanga Māori and te reo Māori.[5]

# Board-Management Relationship Policies

## 2. Global Board-Management Relationship Policy

The board will instruct the principal through written policies that prescribe the organisational Impacts to be achieved, broad operational expectations and organisational situations and actions to be avoided, allowing the principal to use any reasonable interpretation of these policies.

## 2.1 Board authority

A board is responsible for the governance of the school, including setting the policies by which the school is to be controlled and managed. Only officially passed motions of the board are binding on the principal.

### 2.1.1 Rests with the Board

Decisions or instructions of individual board members, officers, or committees are not binding on the principal except in rare instances when the board has specifically authorised such exercise of authority.

### 2.1.2 Requests of Principal need authorisation of the board

In the case of board members or committees requesting information or assistance without board authorisation, the principal can refuse such requests that require, in the principal's opinion, a material amount of staff time or funds, or are disruptive.

## 2.2 Accountability of the Principal

The principal is the board's only link to the operation of the school, so that all authority and accountability of staff is through the principal.

### 2.2.1 Exclusively

The board will never give instructions to persons who report directly or indirectly to the principal.

### 2.2.2 Only Principal manages staff

The board will not manage, either formally or informally, any staff other than the principal.

### 2.2.3 The principal is accountable for school performance

The board will view principal performance as identical to school performance, in so far as it is reasonable to do so, so that school accomplishment of board-stated Impacts and within operational expectations will be viewed as successful principal performance.

## 2.3 Delegation to the Principal

The board will instruct the principal through written policies that prescribe the organisational Impacts to be achieved, broad operational expectations and organisational situations and actions to be avoided, allowing the principal to use any reasonable interpretation of these policies.

### 2.3.1 Delegation to the Principal for achieving Impacts

Impact policies will be developed by the board instructing the principal to achieve specified results, for all students, within resources available to the board.

### 2.3.2 Delegations to the Principal will include delegations

The board's Operational Expectation policies will describe those practices, activities, decisions, and circumstances that would be unacceptable to the board even if they were to be effective. Outside of these operational expectations the principal has freedom to use their discretion in exercising their delegations.

### 2.3.3 Reasonable Interpretation

As long as the principal uses any reasonable interpretation of the board's Impact and Operational Expectation policies, the principal is authorised to establish all further policies, make all decisions, take all actions, establish all practices, and pursue all activities. Such decisions of the principal shall have full force and authority as if decided by the board.

### 2.3.4 Changing delegations

The board may change its Impact and Operational Expectation policies, thereby shifting the boundary between board and principal decision-making.

## 2.4 Monitoring Principal Performance

Systematic and rigorous monitoring of principal performance will be solely against the only expected principal job outputs: organisational accomplishment of board policies on Impacts and organisational operation within the boundaries established in board policies on Operational Expectations.

### 2.4.1 Purpose of Monitoring

Monitoring is simply to determine the degree to which board policies are being met. Information that does not do this will not be considered to be monitoring information.

### 2.4.2 Sources of information

The board will acquire monitoring information by one or more of three methods:

a) By internal report, in which the principal discloses interpretations and compliance information to the board
b) By external report, in which an external, disinterested third party selected by the board assesses compliance with board policies
c) By direct board inspection, in which a designated member or members of the board assess compliance with the appropriate policy criteria.

### 2.4.3 Board Judgements

In every case, the board will judge (a) the reasonableness of the principal's interpretation and (b) whether data demonstrates accomplishment of the interpretation.

### 2.4.4 Reasonable Interpretation

The standard of compliance shall be any reasonable principal interpretation of the board policy being monitored. The board is the final arbiter of reasonableness but will always judge with a "reasonable person" test rather than with an interpretation favoured by board members or by the board as a whole.

### 2.4.5 Frequency of Policy Monitoring

All policies that instruct the principal will be monitored at a frequency and by a method chosen by the board. The board can monitor any policy at any time by any method, but will ordinarily depend on a routine schedule:

| Policy | Method | Frequency |
|--------|--------|-----------|
| Treatment of Learners | Internal | Annually |
| Treatment of Staff | Internal | Annually |
| Financial Planning and Budgeting | Internal | Quarterly |
| Financial Condition and Activities | Internal | Quarterly |
| | External | Annually |
| Emergency principal Succession | Internal | Annually |
| Compensation and Benefits | Internal | Annually |
| | External | Every two years |
| Communication and Support | Direct Inspection | Annually |

## 2.5 Compliance with Statement of National Education and Learning Priorities (NELP)

The board will ensure compliance with the Statement of National Education and Learning Priorities (NELP) as required by law. This includes regularly reviewing and aligning the school's strategic goals with the NELP priorities.

## 2.6 Financial Responsibility

The board will ensure financial responsibility in all aspects of school management, including regular monitoring of financial conditions and activities, as per its statutory obligations.

## 2.7 Inclusive Education

The board is committed to ensuring that the school is inclusive and caters for students with differing needs, in accordance with its legal obligations.

# Operational Expectation Policies

## 3. Global Operational Expectation Policy

The principal shall not cause or allow any school practice, activity, decision, or circumstance that is either unlawful, imprudent, or in violation of their professional standards or commonly accepted business ethics and practices.

## 3.1 Expectations with Students

The school board has an obligation to ensure the wellbeing of children in our care, so they belong, thrive, and achieve. We are committed to the prevention of child abuse and neglect and to the protection of all children. The safety and wellbeing of the child is our top priority. Advice will be sought through appropriate agencies in all cases of suspected or alleged abuse. In relation to students, the principal shall not cause or allow conditions, procedures, or decisions that are contrary to their rights, unsafe, untimely, undignified, or unnecessarily intrusive.

### 3.1.1 Child Safety

The principal will not fail to establish procedures that all employees and school contractors are aware of with respect to child safety that comply with legislation, including the Children's Act 2014, and recognised best practice so that any person in the school who believes that any child or young person who have been or are at risk of being harmed (whether physically, emotionally, or sexually) ill-treated, abused, neglected, or deprived must follow school procedures and report to a senior leader or the school guidance counsellor who may choose to report the matter local police or Oranga Tamariki. The principal shall ensure that mandatory safety checking of staff is carried out in accordance with the Children's Act 2014.

### 3.1.2 Reducing Distress and Minimising Restraint

The principal shall not fail to ensure that the Education (Physical Restraint) Rules 2023, and related guide on understanding distress and minimising restraint, are understood and followed by staff.

### 3.1.3 SPARK Values and Gospel Values

The principal shall not fail to ensure that the school's SPARK values (Self-control, Positivity, Aroha, Respect, Kindness) and gospel values, including manaakitanga and whanaungatanga, are integrated into the school's culture and curriculum, fostering a positive and inclusive learning environment for all students.

### 3.1.4 Holistic Development

The principal shall not fail to implement programs and practices that support the holistic development of students, in line with the school's mission to "live, learn and love with Jesus, being the best we can be."

## 3.2 Expectations with Staff

In relation to staff, the principal will not fail to observe current employment legislation and may not cause or allow conditions that are inconsistent with public sector expectations of a "good employer" or are unfair, undignified, disorganised, or unclear.

### 3.2.1 Good Faith

The principal will maintain a position of good faith in all interactions with staff. The principal shall not fail to:
- Ensure that employees are not discriminated against on other than clearly job-related, individual performance or qualifications.
- Ensure all employees their rights to personal dignity, safety and access to an approved and fair internal grievance process and
- Ensure that staff are aware of their responsibilities as employees of the school to their colleagues and to their employer.
- Provide Protected Disclosure procedures.

### 3.2.2 EEO

The principal shall not fail to develop and execute an EEO programme, that is available to all employees and report to the board on its implementation and compliance.

### 3.2.3 Remuneration and Benefits

The principal should not fail to have employment agreement for all employees. If a position is covered by a collective agreement the provisions that apply to that position as described in the collective agreement must be applied. For positions for which there is not a collective agreement available that an individual agreement is in place.

### 3.2.4 Engagement of Consultants, Contractors and Volunteers

The principal may not enter into any grant or contract arrangements that fail to emphasise primarily the meeting of Impacts and, secondarily, the avoidance of unacceptable means.

### 3.2.5 Professional Development

The principal shall not fail to provide opportunities for staff professional development, particularly in areas that support the school's mission, values, and participation in the Northern Porirua Kāhui Ako|Community of Learning.

## 3.3 Financial Conditions & Activity

In relation to the actual, ongoing financial conditions and activities, the principal shall not cause or allow the development of financial jeopardy or material deviation of actual expenditures from board priorities established in Impact Policies. The principal shall ensure compliance with the Education and Training Act 2020 and the Crown Entities Act 2004 in relation to financial reporting and management.

### 3.3.1 Financial Planning/Budgeting

Budgeting shall not fail to reflect the board's Impact policies, risk financial jeopardy nor fail to show a generally acceptable level of foresight. Thus, the budget should not neglect to:
- Reflect the impacts sought by the board.
- Reflect the priorities as established by the board.
- Comply with the board's requirement of a balanced budget.
- Ensure adequate working capital.
- Demonstrate an appropriate degree of conservatism in all estimates.

### 3.3.2 Financial Condition

The financial viability of the organisation must be protected at all times. Therefore, the principal shall not:
a. Incur unauthorised debt.
b. Violate generally accepted accounting practices or principles.
c. Use tagged funds for purposes other than those approved.
d. Spend more funds than have been allocated in the fiscal year.
e. Fail to ensure all money owed to the school is collected in a timely manner.
f. Fail to make timely payment to staff and other creditors.
g. Sell or purchase unauthorised property.
h. Spend on single items beyond a board-established limit.
i. Fail to ensure that all relevant government returns are completed on time.
j. Fail to ensure that no one person has complete authority over the school's financial transactions.
k. Make any purchase:
   1. Of over $5,000 without having obtained comparative prices and quality.
   2. Of over $10,000 without an adequate review on ongoing costs, value and reliability.

### 3.3.3 Impact Focus of Grants or Contracts

The principal may not enter into any grant or contract arrangements that fail to emphasise primarily the meeting of Impacts and, secondarily, the avoidance of unacceptable means.

## 3.4 Asset Protection

The principal shall not cause or allow school assets to be unprotected, inadequately maintained, or unnecessarily risked. Therefore, the Principal may not:
a) Fail to insure assets.
b) Allow unauthorised personnel to handle funds or school property.
c) Subject plant and equipment to improper wear and tear or insufficient maintenance or inappropriate use.
d) Fail to ensure the implementation of the 10-year property maintenance plan in accordance with Ministry of Education guidelines.
e) Fail to protect intellectual property, information and files from loss or significant damage or unauthorised access or duplication.
f) Receive, process or disburse funds under controls that are insufficient to meet the board-appointed auditor's standards.
g) Invest or hold operating capital in insecure accounts, or in non-interest bearing accounts except where necessary to facilitate ease in operational transactions.

## 3.5 Communication and Support to the Board

The principal shall not cause or allow the board to be uninformed or unsupported in its work.

## 3.6 Emergency Principal Succession

To protect the board from sudden loss of Principal services, the principal shall not permit there to be fewer than two other senior managers sufficiently familiar with board and Principal issues and processes to enable either to take over with reasonable proficiency as an interim successor.

## 3.7 Health & Safety

Students and staff shall have a safe physical and emotional learning environment. The board recognises that everyone is responsible for Health & Safety and that the board, in its role as the Person Conducting a Business or Undertaking (PCBU) under the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015, has the primary responsibility for the health, safety and wellbeing of workers. The board expects the principal and staff to design and implement a Health and Safety system that protects all employees, students, volunteers, contractors and visitors to the school. The principal will provide the board with regular reports to give the board assurance that the school is a safe physically and emotionally place for all students and staff.

### 3.7.1 Workers Participation

The Health & Safety System will ensure workers are regularly made aware of opportunities to participate in improving health and safety including through health and safety representatives and the health and safety committee. The principal will also ensure workers can raise any health and safety concerns directly with them.

### 3.7.2 Managing Risk

The Health & Safety System will include clearly documented procedures for identifying and reporting hazards and risk and systems for managing those risks that may include mitigation or rectification.

### 3.7.3 Property and Equipment

The principal must have systems that ensure that all property and equipment are well-maintained and are in safe condition to use.

### 3.7.4 Emergency Plans including Evacuations

The Health and Safety System will include plans for responding to emergencies affecting the school, including weather-related events, natural disasters and other threats to health and life. The principal is delegated the authority to deal with all aspects of such events including decisions to close the school if it is deemed unsafe to remain open.

### 3.7.5 Health and Wellbeing

The principal will engage closely with staff and students to support a proactive approach to health and wellbeing with specific health and wellbeing programmes.

### 3.7.6 Induction, Training and Information

The principal will not fail to ensure that a programme of induction is provided for all new employees and that staff receive professional development in health and safety that will strengthen the capability of staff to maintain a safe and happy work environment including how to keep themselves as safe as possible.

### 3.7.7 EOTC Provisions

The principal will not fail to ensure that the Health & Safety System will include specific systems for ensuring that all EOTC events including sports events are safe for all participants and appropriately supervised.

## 3.8 Concerns and Complaints

The principal shall not fail to ensure robust procedures to ensure complaints, concerns and incidents are attended to promptly, fairly, respectfully and professionally, and seek to bring effective resolution to all parties concerned.

### 3.8.1 The principal shall not fail to ensure:

a) processes involved observe principles of natural justice;
b) the process for complaints or grievances is clearly communicated and posted on the school website;
c) Staff are empowered to successfully address concerns and complaints, including de-escalating without minimising concerns;
d) advice is given to members of the learning community on how to formulate concerns and complaints;
e) a complainant has previously followed the school's concerns and complaints procedure before a complaint is escalated to board level;
f) advise complainants that, if the principal's action or inaction in relation to a concern or complaint is unfair, unreasonable, or contrary to the facts, board policy or the law, they may make a complaint in those terms to the board.

### 3.8.2 Cultural Sensitivity

The principal shall not fail to ensure that the concerns and complaints process is culturally sensitive and responsive, particularly to the needs of Māori and Pasifika students and their whānau, who make up a significant portion of the school community.

## 3.9 Community Engagement

The principal shall not fail to engage with the school's community, including parents, whānau, and local iwi, to foster strong relationships and support the school's mission and values. The principal shall ensure that this engagement aligns with the board's obligations under Te Tiriti o Waitangi.

### 3.9.1 Cultural Responsiveness

The principal shall not fail to ensure that the school's practices and curriculum are culturally responsive, particularly to the needs of Māori and Pasifika students, who make up 24% of the student population.

### 3.9.2 Catholic Special Character

The principal shall not fail to maintain and enhance the school's Catholic special character in all aspects of school life, in line with the school's state-integrated status and mission.

### 3.9.3 Kāhui Ako Participation

The principal shall not fail to actively participate in and contribute to the Northern Porirua Kāhui Ako|Community of Learning, sharing best practices and working collaboratively to improve student outcomes across the community.

# Governance Culture Policies

## Global Governance Culture Policy

The purpose of the board, on behalf of the Crown and community, is to see to it that the school (a) achieves its desired results for its students (as described in its Impact policies) within the resourcing available to it, (b) avoids unacceptable actions and situations (as prohibited in board Operational Expectation policies) while fulfilling its commitment to Te Tiriti o Waitangi.

## 4.1 Governing Style

The board of St Theresa's School (Plimmerton) emphasises strategic leadership rather than administrative details, maintains a clear distinction of board and staff roles, concentrates on the future rather than the past or present, and is proactive rather than reactive. Our governing style is guided by our Catholic values and the SPARK principles (Self-control, Positivity, Aroha, Respect, Kindness).

### 4.1.1 Group Responsibility

The board will cultivate a sense of group responsibility, reflecting our commitment to whanaungatanga. All members have equal standing, voice, vote, and accountability. The board, not the staff, will be responsible for excellence in governing. The board will be the initiator of policy, not merely a reactor to staff initiatives.

### 4.1.2 Direction through Policy

The board will direct, control, and inspire the school through the careful establishment of broad written policies reflecting the board's values and perspectives, aligned with our mission "To live, learn and love with Jesus, being the best we can be."

The board's written policies will realistically address the broadest levels of all organisational decisions and situations:

- Impact - the impacts, benefits, outcomes; recipients, beneficiaries, impacted groups; and their relative worth in cost or priority
- Operational Expectations - constraints on executive authority that establish the prudence and ethics boundaries within which all executive activity and decisions must take place
- Governance Culture - specification of how the board conceives, carries out, and monitors its own task
- Board-Management Relationship - how power is delegated, and its proper use monitored, the principal's role, authority, and accountability

### 4.1.3 Board Discipline

Board members will adhere to the Code of Conduct for School Board Members issued by the Ministry of Education and enforce upon itself whatever discipline is needed to govern with excellence. This includes regular attendance, thorough preparation for meetings, adherence to policymaking principles, respect of roles, and commitment to continual governance improvement.

### 4.1.4 Board Continuity & Development

The board will ensure continual development, including induction of new members and succession planning. This will include educating board members about the unique context of St Theresa's School, including its Catholic character, SPARK values, and role within the Northern Porirua Kāhui Ako|Community of Learning.

### 4.1.5 Board Self-monitoring

The board will regularly self-monitor its performance against its policies in the Governance Culture and Board-Management Relationship categories.

## 4.2 Board's Role

The board's role is to provide leadership by reflecting the agreed aspirations and expectations of the school community, the Crown, and the Catholic Church in board policy. The board is accountable to the community and the Crown on these key roles and will regularly report on progress and compliance.

The board's primary objectives, as outlined in section 127 of the Education and Training Act 2020, are to ensure:

1. Every student at the school is able to attain their highest possible standard in educational achievement
2. The school is a physically and emotionally safe place for all students and staff
3. The school gives effect to Te Tiriti o Waitangi, including by:
   a. Working to ensure its plans, policies, and local curriculum reflect local tikanga Māori, mātauranga Māori, and te ao Māori
   b. Taking all reasonable steps to make instruction available in tikanga Māori and te reo Māori
   c. Achieving equitable outcomes for Māori students

The board is a continuous entity, regardless of changes in membership, and its policies and decisions continue to exist until such time as they are rescinded or revoked.

### 4.2.1 Connection to Owners

The board will gain an understanding of Crown expectations, Catholic education principles, and the values and aspirations of our diverse community (including 16% Māori and 8% Samoan students) to incorporate these into board policy.

### 4.2.2 Monitoring & Assurance

The board will monitor the effect of its policies through principal reports, third-party monitoring, and direct inspection where warranted, to ensure successful school performance on Impacts and Operational Expectations. The board will regularly monitor and evaluate its performance against the strategic plan and annual implementation plan, as required by section 145 of the Education and Training Act 2020.

### 4.2.3 Board Work Plan

To fulfil its role, the board will follow an annual work plan that:

a) Completes a re-exploration of Impact policies annually
b) Continually improves board performance through education and enriched input and deliberation
c) Reviews other policies as appropriate
d) Engages with the local Catholic parish and Diocese
e) Collaborates with other schools in the Northern Porirua Kāhui Ako|Community of Learning
f) Prepares and submits an annual report, as outlined in section 134 of the Education and Training Act 2020

1. The cycle will conclude each year on the last day of December so that administrative planning and budgeting can be based on accomplishing the annual implementation plan.

2. The cycle will start with the board's development of its agenda for the next year.
   a. Consultations with selected groups in the ownership, or other methods of gaining ownership input, will be determined and arranged in the first quarter, to be held during the balance of the year.
   b. Governance education and education related to ends determination (presentations by futurists, demographers, advocacy groups, staff, and so on) will be arranged in the first quarter, to be held during the balance of the year.
   c. A board member may recommend or request an item for board discussion by submitting the item to the Presiding Member no later than five days before the board meeting.

3. Throughout the year, the board will attend to consent agenda items as expeditiously as possible.

## 4.3 Principal Monitoring

Principal monitoring will be on the agenda if reports have been received since the previous meeting, if plans must be made for direct inspection monitoring, or if arrangements for third-party monitoring must be prepared.

## 4.4 Board Meetings

Board meetings will follow the requirements of the Education (School Boards) Regulations 2020 and part 7 of the Local Government Official Information and Meetings Act 1987. The board may use formal meeting procedures or consensus decision-making as appropriate, ensuring fair participation and clear documentation of decisions.

Specific procedures include:
1. Taking minutes of all board meetings and making them available to the public
2. Ensuring that meetings are open to the public, except when the board has grounds to exclude the public under the Local Government Official Information and Meetings Act 1987
3. Providing public notice of meetings
4. Maintaining a record of all board resolutions

## 4.5 Presiding Member's Role

The presiding member (PM) chairs the board meetings and ensures the integrity of the board's process. The PM is authorised to represent the board to outside parties where appropriate.

### 4.5.1 Presiding Member's Role

The PM will ensure that board meetings focus on issues that clearly belong to the board, as determined by board policy. Deliberations will be fair, open, thorough, timely, orderly, and aligned with the school's Catholic character and SPARK values.

### 4.5.2 Presiding Member's Authority

The PM has the authority to make decisions under the board policies on Governance Culture and Board-Management Relationship, except for:
(a) The employment or termination of the principal and
(b) Areas where the board specifically delegates portions of this authority to others.

4.5.2.1 The PM shall preside over meetings of the board with all the commonly accepted powers of that position.

4.5.2.2 Except as delegated by the board, the PM has no authority to supervise or direct the principal.

4.5.2.3 The PM may, as delegated, represent the board to outside parties in announcing board decisions and confirming the process being undertaken by the school in urgent or critical matters.

4.5.2.4 Under the authority of the board, the PM may delegate any part or all of these responsibilities.

## 4.6 Board Secretary

The role of the board secretary is to ensure the integrity of the board's documents.

### 4.6.1 Board Secretary's Role

While the formal contractual arrangements may sensibly be handled by the principal, the board secretary is accountable to the board (and their key functional relationship is with its presiding member).

The assigned result is to see to it that all board documents and filings are accurate and timely, including the following:

a) Meeting minutes that are an official record of the board's process and actions, particularly its resolutions and adoption of policies (board policies cannot be shown to have been created or altered except through the authority of the minutes).
b) A centrally-organised, accessible and visible collection of current policies, with clear indication of adoption date by the board.
c) Central filing of formal board correspondence.
d) Up-to-date board membership records, including ensuring and filing eligibility declarations, a board membership register (that records phone and email contacts, formal position on the board, membership type (elected, selected, co-opted, ex officio) date membership started, date member left board, expiry of term), and ensuring new board members (including the principal) and new presiding members complete Appendix 2 to update Te Whakaroputanga records.
e) Information portfolio for each board member. Core documents that each board member should be given (Code of conduct, Key legislative requirements, Core policies, Strategic plan, Contacts list...)

## 4.7 Board Committee Principles

Board committees are to be used sparingly, preferably as ad hoc committees to preserve the board functioning as a whole when other methods have been deemed inadequate and so as never to interfere with delegation from board to principal.

This policy applies to any group that is formed by board decision, whether or not it is called a committee. A board committee may include non-board members but must include at least one board member. It does not apply to committees formed under the authority of the principal.

A board committee shall be given written terms of reference that describe constitution, aims and delegations.

### 4.7.1 Board Committee Role

Board committees are to help the board do its job, never to advise or carry out the work of staff. Committees ordinarily will assist the board by preparing policy alternatives and implications for board deliberation. In keeping with the board's broader focus, board committees will normally not have direct dealings with current staff operations.

### 4.7.2 Board Committee Scope

Board committees may not speak or act for the board except when formally given such authority for specific and time-limited purposes. Expectations and authority will be carefully stated in order to prevent conflict with authority delegated to the principal.

### 4.7.3 Board Committees Limit on Authority

Board committees cannot exercise authority over staff. The principal works for the full board and will therefore not be required to obtain the approval of a board committee before an executive action.

## 4.8 Cost of Governance

Because poor governance costs more than learning to govern well, the board will invest in its governance capacity.

### 4.8.1 Resourcing the Board

Board skills, methods, and supports will be sufficient to ensure governing with excellence.

1. Training and retraining will be used liberally to orient new members and candidates for membership, as well as to maintain and increase existing members' skills and understandings.
2. Outside monitoring assistance will be arranged so that the board can exercise confident control over organisational performance. This includes but is not limited to financial audit.
3. Outreach mechanisms will be used as needed to ensure the board's ability to listen to owner viewpoints and values.

### 4.8.2 Board Costs

Costs will be prudently incurred, though not at the expense of endangering the development and maintenance of superior capability. The board will establish its Cost of Governance budget for the next financial year during the month of October.

b. Up to $ in financial year XXXX for audit and other third-party monitoring of organisational performance.
c. Up to $ in financial year XXXX for surveys, focus groups, opinion analyses, and meeting costs.

## 4.9 Health and Safety

The board is committed to ensuring compliance with health and safety requirements, as outlined in the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015. This includes:

1. Providing a safe and healthy work environment for staff, students, and visitors
2. Ensuring that health and safety considerations are integrated into all school activities and decisions
3. Regularly reviewing and updating health and safety policies and procedures
4. Providing appropriate training and resources for staff to carry out their health and safety responsibilities
5. Monitoring and reporting on health and safety performance
6. Continuously improving health and safety practices within the school
